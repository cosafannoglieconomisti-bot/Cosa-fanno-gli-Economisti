## YouTube Description

The study "From Weber to Kafka: Political Instability and the Overproduction of Laws" by Gabriele Gratton, Luigi Guiso, Claudio Michelacci and Massimo Morelli, published in the American Economic Review in 2021, explains how a wave of political instability can push less competent politicians to churn out too many low-quality laws to signal that they are reformers: with over 75,000 Italian laws (1948–2016) from Normattiva, after the shift from the First to the Second Republic words of legislation per quarter jump from ~261,000 to ~475,000, pages per law from 3.7 to 12.8 and bureaucratic efficiency (ICRG) falls — too many rules clog the bureaucracy and can tip the system into a Kafkaesque state.
